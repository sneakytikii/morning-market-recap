Morning Market Recap

Friday, July 31, 2026 · Closing Report Markets closed Trading restarts Monday, 9:30 AM New York time

In plain English

Friday was a good day overall, but a lopsided one. Amazon jumped 15% after a very strong quarter, while Apple fell 7% after a weak one. The market went up because the winners were bigger than the losers — not because everything went up.

The bigger story of July was computer-chip stocks falling hard. The overall market ended the month roughly flat, but your chip fund SOXL lost 57% of its value in those four weeks.

What matters now is interest rates, not company profits. The Federal Reserve left rates unchanged on July 29 and signalled it is in no hurry to cut them. When rates on government bonds go up — and the 30-year bond just hit its highest level in 19 years — investors get a safe alternative to shares, which pulls share prices down.

S&P 500 — the whole US market

7,489.72 ▲ +0.70%

This one number tracks the 500 largest American companies. It is the simplest answer to "how did the market do today?" On Friday it went up 0.70%. For the year so far it is up about 9.4%.

This week about +1% All of July roughly flat 2026 so far about +9.4% Fear gauge 15.99, calm

What happened

  • Jul 31Up, but only a few companies did the work. Amazon's jump outweighed a 7% fall in Apple.
  • Jul 31Government bond rates jumped. The 10-year reached 4.73%, the highest in over a year. The 30-year hit 5.21% — the highest since 2007.
  • Jul 29The Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged, and three of its officials disagreed with that decision — an unusually large split.
  • Jun 17The Fed's own forecast was raised back in June: officials now expect rates to end 2026 between 3.6% and 4.1%, up from 3.25%–3.75%. In short, fewer rate cuts than people had hoped.

Reasons it could go up

Company profits are coming in strong, and the big technology firms just committed to enormous spending. The fear gauge is low, meaning investors are relaxed.

Reasons it could go down

Government bonds now pay 5.21% with far less risk. When safe returns get that good, shares look less attractive by comparison.

What I'd watch: two dates. The jobs report on August 7 and the inflation report on August 12. If inflation comes in hot while bond rates are already this high, that is the combination most likely to knock the market down.

QQQ — the 100 biggest tech companies

$687.99 ▲ +0.65%

A single fund holding the 100 largest non-bank companies on the Nasdaq — mostly technology. Buying one share gives you a slice of all of them. It barely moved this week, but that calm surface hides very large moves underneath.

This week +0.55% Below 1-year high 8.1% Above 1-year low 24.7% Year's range $551.68 – $748.65

What happened

  • Jul 31Opened high, then drifted down all day. It started at $692.11 and finished at $687.99 — lower than where it opened, even though it still counts as an up day.
  • Jul 31Amazon rose 15%. Its cloud-computing division grew 36.7% in a year, its fastest in four and a half years.
  • Jul 31Apple fell 7% because sales of services and sales in China were weaker than expected. This was the single biggest drag.
  • Jul 30Microsoft rose about 15% on strong cloud results, which pushed the whole fund up 3.3% that day.

Reasons it could go up

The two biggest cloud businesses both beat expectations and promised heavy investment. The riskiest moment — earnings season — is now past.

Reasons it could go down

One company down 7% and another up 15% in the same week means investors are being very picky. That is usually a sign of an unsettled market.

What I'd watch: whether it keeps opening high and closing low. That pattern — buyers losing their nerve during the day — is the thing to watch for. One day means nothing; three days in a row would matter.

Nvidia — the AI chip maker

$200.75 ▲ +2.93%

Nvidia makes the chips that power artificial intelligence. It is one of the most valuable companies on earth at $4.86 trillion. It rose nearly 3% on Friday — but it is still down for the week, and 15% below its best price of the past year.

This week down 2.94% Below 1-year high 15.1% Company value $4.86 trillion Price vs profits 30.7× Next earnings August 26

What happened

  • Jul 31Climbed back above $200 after big tech firms promised heavy spending on AI equipment — Nvidia's customers, in other words.
  • Jul 27Dropped about 5% on worries about "circular financing" — the concern that Nvidia's customers are partly funded by Nvidia itself, which would make its sales look better than they really are. Apple overtook it as the world's most valuable company that day.
  • JulyThe US restarted export licences for its H20 chip to China, reopening a market that had been closed. Genuinely good news that got little attention.
  • JulyA multi-billion-dollar partnership with Korea's SK Group was announced, but it does not start until 2027 — no effect on this year's sales.

Reasons it could go up

At 30.7 times profits it is not wildly expensive for a company growing this fast. China is reopening, and its customers have now publicly committed to spending the money.

Reasons it could go down

The "circular financing" worry questions whether the sales are genuine. That is the one criticism that would justify a much lower share price.

What I'd watch: August 26, when Nvidia reports its results. Everything else is noise until then. Going in, the setup looks better than the share price suggests, because its customers have already confirmed they are spending.

SOXL — the triple-risk chip fund

$114.72 flat

Read this one carefully — it is by far the riskiest thing you own. SOXL is built to move three times as much as chip stocks do, every single day. If chip stocks rise 2%, it rises about 6%. If they fall 2%, it falls about 6%. In July it lost 57% of its value while the overall market was flat.

This week down 16.2% All of July down 57% 2026 so far still up 173% Below 1-year high 62% Rise needed to recover +163%

What happened

  • Jul 31A full round trip. It opened at $129.90, climbed to $133.88, fell to $112.42, and finished at $114.72 — almost exactly where it started, after a swing of roughly 16%.
  • Jul 30Rose 24.71% in a single day on Microsoft's results. That one day is the only reason the week was not far worse.
  • Jul 29Fell 16.02% in a single day, while ordinary chip stocks fell only 5.38%. This is the triple effect working against you.
  • JulyChip shares fell across the board: Intel down 21% over seven days, Micron down 13% in one day. The triggers were SK Hynix missing its forecasts, Samsung delaying its new memory chip, and unease about the $489 billion of debt raised for AI projects this year.

Reasons it could go up

It is still up 173% for the year. If the fall was about prices being too high rather than the businesses being broken, this recovers faster than anything else you own.

Reasons it could go down

Because it resets every day, choppy markets cost you money even if prices end up where they started. Six recent days went −13%, −6%, −15%, −16%, +25%, flat — and the fund still lost badly.

What I'd watch — and the one thing I would genuinely flag: this is a tool for short trades, not something to hold onto. Because of the daily reset, a bumpy market drains it whichever direction things end up going: July cost 57% while the wider market went nowhere. If you want to bet on chips recovering before August 26, owning Nvidia itself does that with far less damage from the ups and downs. To get back to its old high it needs to rise 163%.

Reasons it could go up

Double-digit sales growth, fast-growing online business, and loyal paying members. It is the one holding here that does not depend on artificial intelligence.

Reasons it could go down

At 47.9 times profits it is expensive, and it pays only 0.62% in dividends while a government bond pays 5.21% with no risk.

What I'd watch: on August 5, look at the growth rate at existing stores, not the headline sales number. June showed that the market will sell the shares even on a good month if growth is slowing down.

Where these numbers come from, and what to be careful about

The prices

All prices are the official closing prices from Friday, July 31, 2026, checked against three separate sources: StockAnalysis, Yahoo Finance and Google Finance. Where a figure says "about" or "roughly", it was calculated from those closing prices rather than taken from a source directly.

One number I could not fully confirm

SOXL's Friday change is the single figure I could not get three sources to agree on. All three report $114.72 as the closing price — exactly the same as Thursday — while the trading record for the day shows a range of $112.42 to $133.88. A sharp rise followed by a full retreat would explain it, but please treat that particular daily percentage as unconfirmed. Everything else on this page matched across at least two sources.

These are not predictions

Nothing here tells you what will happen next. Nobody can do that, and you should be suspicious of anyone who claims otherwise. What each section gives you instead is the case for going up, the case for going down, and the specific dated event that will settle the argument. This is information only, not financial advice, and it takes no account of how much you own, what you paid, when you need the money, or your tax position.
