Today · Monday, August 17오늘 · 8월 17일 월요일

The chip rally that started before the bell has kept going, and by late morning it had split the day in two. The broad market is drifting a little lower — the S&P 500 is down about a quarter of a percent as borrowing rates and oil edge up — while the chip makers have a big day. The chip fund SOXL is up about 6%, Nvidia is up about half a percent, the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ is roughly flat, and Costco is down about 1%. Behind the chip move, Micron has climbed above $1,000 a share, and a report that the AI company Anthropic's revenue has grown about fourteen-fold in a year gave the whole group a push. Tomorrow before the open, Home Depot starts the week's store-chain results, and the Federal Reserve's July meeting minutes come Wednesday at 11 in the morning Pacific time.

개장 전에 시작된 반도체 상승세가 계속 이어져, 오전이 지나는 사이 하루가 둘로 갈라졌습니다. 시장 전체는 금리와 유가가 조금씩 오르는 가운데 살짝 밀려 S&P 500은 0.25% 정도 내려 있고, 반도체는 큰 하루를 보내고 있습니다. 반도체 펀드 SOXL은 약 6% 올랐고, 엔비디아는 0.5% 정도 올랐으며, 나스닥100 펀드 QQQ는 거의 제자리, 코스트코는 약 1% 내렸습니다. 반도체가 오르는 데에는 마이크론이 한 주당 1,000달러 위로 올라선 것과, AI 회사 앤스로픽의 매출이 1년 새 약 14배로 늘었다는 보도가 힘이 됐습니다. 내일 개장 전에는 홈디포가 이번 주 유통업체 실적의 첫 순서로 나서고, 수요일 서부 시각 오전 11시에는 연방준비제도의 7월 회의 의사록이 나옵니다.

The market begins the new week a small step below its record. On Friday the S&P 500 slipped 0.17% to 7,785.76 — one day after the highest close in its history — because two readings on the American shopper came in weak: July retail sales fell 0.6%, the biggest monthly drop since May 2025, and the University of Michigan's survey of how households feel fell to 51 from 55.2. The detail worth sitting with is inside Friday's numbers. Households told the Michigan survey they expect prices to rise 4.3% over the next year, up from 4.2% — people spending less while bracing for higher prices — and that mix pushed borrowing rates up on a day of bad news: the 10-year government rate to about 4.70%, the 30-year to 5.27%, with oil back near $88.50 a barrel. Yet the market's fear gauge closed at 14.25, its lowest of 2026 — investors read the day as softening, not sinking. The week's other lesson came in two identical chapters: Cisco and Applied Materials each reported the best results in their history, and each fell in the full session that followed — 8.40% and 5.12%. Nothing was wrong with either set of numbers; both shares had already risen a long way on exactly those expectations.

Reasons it could go up

The market itself is remarkably calm: on a day of bad news the fear gauge closed at 14.25, its lowest of 2026 — investors are relaxed rather than braced. Inflation at the wholesale level did not rise at all in July, and Friday's weak data nudged the odds of a September rate rise down, to about one in three. The index has now climbed three weeks in a row, its longest run since May, and sits within a fraction of its record. And the case against it rests, so far, on just two survey-heavy readings — the big store chains report their own results this week, and those are hard numbers that could tell a better story about the shopper.

Reasons it could go down

A price this close to a record means the good news is already paid for, and last week showed the two ways that can hurt. First, the shopper: people spent less in July and told the Michigan survey they expect prices to rise 4.3% over the next year — spending less while bracing for higher prices is the mix that pushed borrowing rates up on a bad-news day, the 10-year to about 4.7% and the 30-year to 5.27%. Long-term government bonds now pay about 5.27% with far less risk than shares. Second, the pattern: two companies reported record results and their shares fell anyway — Cisco down 8.4% Thursday, Applied Materials down 5.1% Friday. Oil is climbing again too, back near $88.50, with the Strait of Hormuz still effectively shut.
