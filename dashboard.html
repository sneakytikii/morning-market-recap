Today · Thursday, October 22오늘 · 10월 22일 목요일

The selling that started this morning has gotten worse, and it is still aimed at the technology shares. The chip-equipment maker Lam Research said last night that some parts are in short supply, and investors took that as a warning for the whole chip group, which is down around 6% — while worry about the cost of the AI build-out keeps weighing on big names like Meta. Of the five here, Nvidia is down about 4%, the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ about 2.5%, and the chip fund SOXL, which moves at three times the group's size, around 18% — Costco, up about 1% and back above $1,000, is where money is sheltering, and bonds are rising too. Tonight after the close come Intel's results, and the Fed decides on rates next Wednesday.

오전에 시작된 매도세가 갈수록 더 깊어지고 있고, 여전히 기술주를 겨냥하고 있습니다. 반도체 장비 회사 Lam Research가 어젯밤 일부 부품이 부족하다고 말했는데, 투자자들이 이를 반도체 업계 전체에 대한 경고로 받아들이면서 반도체주가 무리 지어 6% 안팎 내렸습니다 — AI 구축 비용에 대한 걱정도 Meta 같은 큰 회사들을 계속 누르고 있습니다. 보유 종목 중에서는 Nvidia가 4%가량, 나스닥100 펀드 QQQ가 2.5%가량 내렸고, 업계 움직임의 세 배 크기로 움직이는 반도체 펀드 SOXL은 18% 안팎 내렸습니다 — Costco는 1%가량 올라 $1,000 위로 다시 올라서며 돈이 피하는 곳이 됐고, 채권도 오르고 있습니다. 오늘 장 마감 후에는 Intel 실적이 나오고, 다음 주 수요일에는 연준이 금리를 결정합니다.

Wednesday, in review수요일 장 돌아보기

Yesterday the repair reached the technology shares — and after the close, the report cards poured in. The S&P 500 rose 0.58% on Wednesday to 7,757.17, a third straight gain that put it back within about 0.8% of its record. This time the chip shares led, jumping about 2% after Nvidia said six of the world's biggest investment firms will help raise over $500 billion of outside money to finance the AI build-out. The fear gauge closed at 16.68, the calmest since the chip selling began two weeks ago.

어제는 수리가 기술주까지 닿았습니다 — 그리고 장 마감 뒤, 성적표가 쏟아져 들어왔습니다. S&P 500은 수요일 0.58% 올라 7,757.17로 마쳤고, 사흘 연속 상승으로 기록의 약 0.8% 안쪽까지 돌아왔습니다. 이번에는 반도체 주식이 이끌었습니다. 엔비디아가 세계 최대 투자회사 여섯 곳이 AI 건설에 들어갈 외부 자금 5,000억 달러 이상을 모으는 일에 함께한다고 밝히자 약 2% 뛰었습니다. 공포 지수는 2주 전 반도체 매도가 시작된 뒤 가장 차분한 16.68로 마감했습니다.

Then came the grading — and it is this morning's story. Five heavyweights reported after the close: Tesla, Meta, IBM, Texas Instruments and Lam Research. Every one of them beat expectations, and this morning most of their shares are falling anyway, because the market is reading the spending plans, not the report cards. Meta raised its AI budget to as much as $145 billion this year and created a new class of shares it can sell to pay for it; IBM's chief said customers increasingly ask whether AI spending earns its keep; Tesla's profit fell by nearly half even as sales held up. Chip shares are taking the hardest hit — and whatever chips do, SOXL does at about three times the size.

The standing warnings have not moved. Four of your five are the same chip bet, and SOXL triples whatever chips do each day — it needs roughly +193% to see its old high again, and this week it lost about 6.6% while Nvidia went roughly nowhere. The Fed decides on rates next Wednesday; most economists expect no change, but most also still see more rises ahead. And oil is back near the top of the page: the global price touched its highest since May overnight after attacks in the Middle East, and dearer fuel eventually lands in the inflation numbers. Nobody can tell you how any of this resolves — the honest reading is that the argument over who pays for AI is being settled one report at a time, and tonight it is Intel's turn.

Reasons it could go up

Three straight gains have repaired nearly all of October's damage — the record now sits about 0.8% overhead — and the week's earnings receipts were, on the plain numbers, good: Tesla, Meta, IBM, Texas Instruments, Lam Research and Lockheed Martin all beat expectations, and Texas Instruments said data-centre demand is still growing. The calm has held where it matters: the fear gauge closed at 16.68, its lowest since the selling began, and the ten-year rate, near 5.13%, is well below its 5.33% peak — this morning money moved into bonds, nudging it lower still. The market asked for proof the AI boom is real; the quarter-by-quarter numbers keep providing it.

Reasons it could go down

Every one of this week's big reporters beat expectations, and most were sold anyway — that is a market worried about the bill, not the business. Meta raised its AI spending plan to as much as $145 billion this year and created a new class of shares to sell to help pay for it; IBM's chief said clients increasingly ask whether AI spending is getting out of control; Tesla's profit fell by nearly half even as sales held up. Oil is the other weight: the global price is at its highest since May after attacks in the Middle East, and dearer fuel feeds the same inflation that already has most economists expecting more Fed rises.
