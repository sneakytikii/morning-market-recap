The week, in review지난 한 주 돌아보기

The week asked one question — is the giant spending on AI worth it? — and the market's answer was to punish nearly everyone who reported. Four of the biggest AI spenders turned in their quarterly report cards. Only Microsoft, the one that spent less than forecast, was rewarded. Alphabet lost about $482 billion on Thursday — the biggest one-day fall in its history. Amazon was marked down overnight and largely forgiven by Friday's close. And Intel, after warning that the PC slowdown will last into next year, fell 28.6% on Friday — its worst day since 1974 — handing the whole market its worst day since June on October's final session.

이번 주는 질문 하나를 물었습니다 — AI에 쓰는 그 막대한 돈이 값어치를 하는가? — 그리고 시장의 답은 발표한 거의 모두를 벌주는 것이었습니다. 가장 큰 AI 지출 기업 넷이 분기 성적표를 냈습니다. 상을 받은 것은 예상보다 적게 쓴 Microsoft 하나뿐입니다. Alphabet은 목요일에 약 4,820억 달러를 잃었습니다 — 역사상 가장 큰 하루 하락입니다. Amazon은 밤사이 감점됐다가 금요일 마감까지 대체로 용서받았습니다. 그리고 PC 둔화가 내년까지 간다고 경고한 Intel은 금요일에 28.6% 내려 — 1974년 이후 최악의 하루 — 10월의 마지막 거래일에 시장 전체에 6월 이후 최악의 하루를 안겼습니다.

What the week cost each of your five depended on how much chip was inside it. Costco, the only one with none, was the only riser: up about 0.9%, back above the $1,000 line. The broad S&P 500 lost only about 0.6%, because for most of the week the money leaving technology moved into steadier corners — health care, property, household staples — rather than out of the market. The technology fund QQQ lost about 2%; Nvidia lost about 3%, even though every big customer confirmed the demand for its chips; and SOXL, which triples the chip index daily, lost a quarter of its value on Friday alone and finished the week down about 7.8% — the early-week rally had been that large. October's final scores tell the same story: Costco up about 11.2%, the S&P 500 down about 0.9%, QQQ down 6.7% — its worst month since March 2025 — and SOXL down about 47.9%, nearly half.

Reasons it could go up

A week that contained the two worst single-stock crashes of the season cost this index just 0.6% — the rest of the market kept absorbing the blows. Reasons it could go up

Every one of its three biggest customers has now reported, and all three raised or held the budgets that buy its chips — Alphabet significantly higher next year, Amazon at a record $220 billion with cloud growth projected above 40% into 2028, Microsoft growing 48% with demand exceeding supply. Intel’s AI data-centre business grew 78% even as its PC side collapsed, the memory makers see shortage for years, and banks financed $63 billion of new AI computing this month. The order book, told entirely by other people, keeps getting bigger. At roughly 26 times the past year’s profits — the cheapest of the year — the price asks less of the future than it has since the spring, the record $150 billion buyback stands behind it, and China sales still sit near zero in the company’s own forecasts: anything Washington concedes on exports remains nearly pure upside. Its own report lands November 17, with expectations now lowered for it.

Reasons it could go down

The market has now spent two full weeks teaching Nvidia’s customers that buying fewer of its chips is rewarded and buying more is punished — and customers eventually learn. Microsoft was paid for trimming; Alphabet, Amazon and Intel surrendered hundreds of billions between them. Friday’s coverage named the next step out loud: investors fear the big spenders will be forced to pull back in 2027, and if even one of the three giants shifts to Microsoft’s playbook, that is Nvidia’s revenue being cut in public. The shares closed at their lowest since early August, the Justice Department is asking about the Hugging Face purchase, a December rate rise is still priced near 70% — and the company has nothing of its own to say until November 17, with AMD’s Tuesday report able to swing the whole group either way first. The standing reminder about the tripled fund: Friday cost Nvidia 4.22% — and SOXL 24.75%.
