The one thing that actually mattered was the bond market. A far-stronger-than-expected business survey and a badly received $70 billion five-year bond sale pushed rates up midweek, most Fed officials still expect another rise — the next decision is October 28 — and every share is priced against what those bonds pay. Wednesday, the week’s one real fall, showed what that pressure does; the other four days showed the market absorbing it. The set-pieces resolved quietly: the summit ended with warm toasts, a tariff truce extended to January 10, and no announcement on chips or export rules in either direction — the question that matters most to four of your five simply stays open. Costco beat expectations on profits and revenue, the market slept on it, then gave the shares their best day of the week on Friday, up 2.9%. And oil, which jumped midweek after missiles were fired at Saudi Arabia, fell back on reports of US-Iran talks and ended the week around $92 a barrel, below the $100 line it had held for a fortnight.

Reasons it could go up

The test kept getting harder all week, and the market kept passing it. The ten-year rate rose about 0.16 percentage points and set two record-since-2007 closes — and the index still gained about 1.2%, with all five of your positions up together for the first week this month. The three big bond sales are done; the summit ended in friendly words and a truce extended to January 10; Costco’s beat was rewarded by Friday. The record is about 0.7% away — an ordinary good day reaches it — and the year’s gain is about 13.1%, earned through a rate rise, an oil spike and a chip scare.

Reasons it could go down

Rates rose all week and shares rose anyway — one of those two usually gives. The ten-year finished at about 5.17%, up from 5.01% in five days; the thirty-year reached about 5.49%, reported as the dearest since 2004; and most Fed officials still expect another rise, with the next decision October 28. The week’s calm rests on talks that have produced no deal: if the Iran negotiations stall, oil and rates take back what the reports gave.
