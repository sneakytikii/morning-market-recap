Tuesday, in review화요일 장 돌아보기

Yesterday the market caught its breath and kept everything it won on Monday. The S&P 500 finished flat — 0.06 points lower — after trading within 0.2% of its record during the day, and the Nasdaq edged up 0.45% to 27,244.28, a second straight all-time high. The chip run did not pause with it: the sector rose a sixth straight day, led by memory again — Micron rose 5%, a week before its own results. Of your five: SOXL +7.06%, QQQ +0.81% — within 30 cents of its record during the day — NVDA +0.66%, COST +0.10%, SPX flat.

The pressure keeps coming off, and the reasons are still appointments, not events. Oil fell a fifth straight day — Brent to about $98, American crude around $90 — after Iran was reported to have signalled it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within days if military pressure eased, and the President told the United Nations he expects any Iran deal only after the November election. The ten-year borrowing rate held at about 4.96%, a second day below 5%, and the first of this week's three bond sales passed quietly. One genuinely new thing surfaced: the President said he has pushed inside his administration to stop American diesel exports — diesel is at a record $6.53 a gallon — and refinery shares fell as the idea circulated, Valero by about 4%. No decision yet; he says one comes quickly. A market this calm about diplomacy is a market that will notice quickly if the diplomacy stalls.

Tomorrow is the day this whole week has been pointing at, and it carries two tests at once. After the close, Costco reports full results — the only one of your five reporting, priced at about 45 times profits on the strength of a margin nobody has seen yet. And during the day the President meets Xi Jinping at the White House — Xi arrives in Washington today, his first state visit in 11 years — with artificial intelligence, the tariff truce and rare earths on the published agenda. Remember what is riding on that room: chip shares have risen six days partly on hopes for it, four of your five are the same chip bet, and SOXL triples whatever the sector decides the outcome is worth. The five-day scoreboard shows what an unbroken run does — SOXL about +48.5%, more than a simple triple of the index's +14.8%, because the daily reset compounds in your favour on a straight road. The same reset collects on a winding one. It took 57% in July. Tomorrow decides which road this is.

Reasons it could go up
