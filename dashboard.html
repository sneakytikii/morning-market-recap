Today · Friday, August 7오늘 · 8월 7일 금요일

The July jobs report landed before the open and it was weak: instead of adding the 80,000 or so jobs forecasters expected, the economy lost 23,000 last month, and May and June were revised down too — though the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1%. Shares rose anyway, because investors read soft hiring as making an interest-rate cut more likely, and the long-term bond rates that took 464 points off the Dow yesterday eased back toward 4.6%. Of your five, the chip fund SOXL is up about 5%, Nvidia and QQQ are each up about 1%, and Costco is close to flat. The S&P 500 is back up around the level where it set its record on Tuesday. Next on the calendar is the inflation report on Wednesday, August 12.

Thursday, in review목요일 장 돌아보기

Yesterday the Dow fell 464 points, or 0.85%, ending five straight gains, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.18% and the Nasdaq was almost unchanged. The cause came from outside the stock market. Iran published draft conditions for shipping through the Strait of Hormuz that fell well short of reopening it, US crude rose 2.8%, and long-term interest rates rose with it.

Your five had a strange morning. Chip shares fell hard at the open after the memory-chip maker Sandisk forecast weaker sales — it dropped as much as 14% — and then climbed back all afternoon. SOXL travelled from $120.75 to $140.00 and finished up 0.20%, almost exactly where it started. Nvidia ended a five-day winning run with a 0.10% slip. Costco rose 0.76%, the market's first answer to Wednesday's July sales figures.

The second thing is that the Strait of Hormuz has not gone quiet: Iran struck targets there overnight, and oil pushed above $78 a barrel before easing back to about $77.

Reasons it could go up

Company profits are coming in strong, and the big technology firms have committed to enormous spending. Even after Thursday's jump, oil is still about 9% cheaper than it was a week ago, which takes pressure off prices generally. The fear gauge fell again, to 15.15, meaning investors are relaxed rather than braced.

Reasons it could go down

Long-term government bonds pay about 5.2% with far less risk, and that rate went back up on Thursday. When safe returns get that good, shares look less attractive by comparison. Thursday also showed how thin the ground is: one publication out of Iran about shipping lanes was enough to lift oil 2.8%, push bond rates up and knock 464 points off the Dow.
