Today · Tuesday, August 25오늘 · 8월 25일 화요일

Futures point modestly higher this morning, with the Nasdaq side leading — the chip shares look like they want to steady after yesterday. Nvidia is quoted about 1% higher before the bell, though early quotes are thin and it has fallen seven days running. Nothing among your five reported overnight, and today's calendar is genuinely light: consumer confidence and new home sales at 7 this morning Pacific time, then a government bond auction in the afternoon. Oil is a little lower again. The week's real business is tomorrow — the inflation report before the open and Nvidia's results after the close — so today mostly answers one question: whether the market wants to buy the chip shares back before that number, or wait for it.

오늘 아침 선물은 소폭 올라 있고, 나스닥 쪽이 앞섭니다. 반도체 주식들이 어제의 하락 뒤 자리를 잡으려는 모습입니다. 엔비디아는 개장 전 약 1% 높게 호가되고 있지만, 이른 시각 호가는 거래가 적어 얇고 이 종목은 일곱 거래일 연속 내린 상태입니다. 보유 다섯 종목과 관련해 밤사이 나온 발표는 없었고, 오늘 일정은 정말 한산합니다. 서부 시각 오늘 오전 7시에 소비자 신뢰지수와 신규 주택 판매가 나오고, 오후에는 국채 입찰이 있습니다. 유가는 오늘도 조금 내려 있습니다. 이번 주의 진짜 일정은 내일입니다. 개장 전 물가 보고서와 장 마감 뒤 엔비디아 실적이지요. 그래서 오늘이 답하는 것은 사실상 하나입니다. 시장이 그 숫자를 보기 전에 반도체 주식을 다시 사려 하는지, 아니면 기다리는지입니다.

Monday, in review월요일 장 돌아보기

Yesterday the market split in two. The chip shares were sold hard while almost everything else held up — NVDA −2.91%, QQQ −1.00%, SOXL −7.83%, but the S&P 500 gave up only 0.28% to 7,652.86, the Dow actually rose 140 points, and Costco climbed 2.50% to $971.40, the only one of your five to rise. Nvidia has now fallen seven sessions in a row, its longest losing run in four years — and it reports its own results tomorrow.

어제 시장은 둘로 갈렸습니다. 반도체 주식은 세게 팔렸지만 나머지는 대부분 버텼습니다. NVDA −2.91%, QQQ −1.00%, SOXL −7.83%였는데 S&P 500은 0.28%만 내려 7,652.86이 됐고, 다우존스는 오히려 140포인트 올랐으며, 코스트코는 2.50% 올라 971.40달러가 됐습니다. 보유 다섯 중 유일한 상승이었습니다. 엔비디아는 이제 일곱 거래일 연속 내렸습니다. 4년 만에 가장 긴 하락 행진이고, 자체 실적은 내일 발표합니다.

The reason the chips were sold is new, and it is worth understanding, because it is the first hard number in a story that had been running on mood. Reports said Nvidia has told customers that servers built around its AI chips will cost more than 15% more from early next year — because the memory chips inside them have become so expensive. That is a real bill, and only two people can pay it: the companies buying the servers, or Nvidia out of its own profit. The market marked down both ends of the trade the same day — Micron fell 5.83% — and the answer arrives with Nvidia's results tomorrow.

반도체가 팔린 이유는 새로운 것이고, 알아 둘 만합니다. 분위기로만 굴러가던 이야기에 처음으로 단단한 숫자가 붙었기 때문입니다. 엔비디아가 고객사들에게 자사 AI 반도체를 넣은 서버 가격을 내년 초부터 15% 넘게 올린다고 알렸다는 보도입니다. 그 안에 든 메모리 반도체 값이 너무 비싸졌기 때문입니다. 이것은 실제로 치러야 할 비용이고, 낼 수 있는 사람은 둘뿐입니다. 서버를 사는 회사들이거나, 자기 이익에서 떼는 엔비디아이거나요. 시장은 같은 날 그 거래의 양쪽 끝을 모두 깎았고(마이크론은 5.83% 하락), 답은 내일 엔비디아 실적과 함께 나옵니다.

Two structural things showed up plainly. The first is the four-way overlap this page always flags: one story about chip costs produced −2.91%, −7.83%, −1.00% and −0.28% on the same afternoon, in that order of severity, while the one holding with no chips in it went the other way entirely. The second is SOXL's daily reset: the chip fund fell 2.67% and SOXL fell 7.83%, and getting back to its 1-year high now needs a rise of about 172%, against 99% a week ago Monday. Elsewhere the news was better than it has been in a while — long-term rates eased off their 19-year high, and oil fell about 2% on the day the toughest Iran sanctions yet were announced. The dated tests ahead: the inflation report and Nvidia's results tomorrow, and the new Fed chair's first Jackson Hole speech on Friday at 7 in the morning Pacific time.

Reasons it could go up

Monday is the encouraging version of a bad day for chips: the AI group was marked down again and the whole index gave up only 0.28%, because the money leaving technology went to other American companies rather than out of the market — the Dow rose 140 points. The bond market helped for the first time in a while, with long rates easing off their 19-year high after a report that the Treasury may fund its bigger bond buying from its own $1 trillion account, and those bigger buybacks actually begin September 9. Oil fell about 2% even as the toughest Iran sanctions yet were announced, which takes pressure off the inflation that rates are built from. And the economy underneath keeps testing well: Friday's business survey was the strongest since April 2022, jobless claims fell to 206,000, and most of the store chains beat.

Reasons it could go down

The market's leadership is still being taken apart, and Monday gave the selling a concrete reason rather than a mood: Nvidia has reportedly told customers that AI servers will cost more than 15% more from early next year because memory has got so expensive. That is a cost the AI buildout has to absorb somewhere, and the index's biggest company reports tomorrow into exactly that question, having fallen seven days running. Long-term rates eased on a news report, not on a decision — a 30-year government bond still pays over 5%, and that competes with every expensive share in this index. Walmart's warning from Thursday still stands: the country's biggest retailer says its customers are slowing down. And the trade fight with Canada widened again — a threat to raise tariffs on all Canadian cars, trucks, parts and steel to 50% in January, with Canada's own answer set for September 8.
