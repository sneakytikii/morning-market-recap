Today · Thursday, November 12오늘 · 11월 12일 목요일

Nvidia said before the bell that Jensen Huang has resigned as chief executive, effective at once, with the finance chief Colette Kress running the company while the board looks for a successor. The shares opened about 13% lower and were down as much as 14% in the first minutes, then most of that came back — by late morning Nvidia was down about 5%. The rest of the market barely moved: the S&P 500 and QQQ are close to flat, the Dow is a shade higher, the chip fund SOXL is back to about even after opening about 9% lower, and Costco is up about half a per cent, having touched its highest price in a year this morning. AMD and Broadcom are each up about 5%, which the day's reporting puts down to an analyst upgrade and to what Nvidia's upheaval might open up for its rivals. Still to come: Cisco and Applied Materials report after tonight's close, and Nvidia's own quarterly figures land next Tuesday after the close, presented by its interim chief.

Wednesday, in review수요일 장 돌아보기

Yesterday every one of your five went up, and one company's results were the reason. The board below shows Wednesday's closing prices — the last completed session. Wednesday's scores: SOXL +17.78%, NVDA +2.87%, QQQ +2.31%, COST +2.01% and SPX +1.31%. CoreWeave, which rents out computing power for artificial intelligence, had reported after Tuesday's close that sales rose 168% and that its contracted order book had more than doubled to about $132 billion; its shares rose about a quarter and pulled the whole chip group with them. The rise was wide, not narrow: ten of the eleven parts of the market finished higher, technology best at 3.23%, rising shares beat falling ones on both American exchanges, and the index set 17 new one-year highs and no new lows. Every one of the thirty companies in the main chip index closed up. The fear gauge fell about 10%, to 17.49, its calmest reading since November 4. The bond market was shut all day for Veterans Day, so the interest rates that did the damage on Tuesday simply could not move.

Then, after the close, the story that is setting this morning — and it is about one man, not about chips. The Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia's board is preparing to remove Jensen Huang as chief executive, and minutes later the New York Times reported a second affair, this one with an Nvidia executive who runs its healthcare business. The board met on Wednesday evening and no decision has been announced. Nvidia did not answer requests for comment. The shares fell sharply in after-hours trading and are down roughly 7% to 8% before this morning's bell — below anything they have closed at in the past year. The size of it: about $310 billion of Nvidia's value went between the Friday before its board announced an investigation and Wednesday's close, and this morning's price would carry that past $600 billion, which is the figure the day's reporting uses. Why it reaches so much of this page: Nvidia is owned outright, is the largest single piece of both SPX and QQQ, and is tripled inside SOXL. What has not changed is the demand. No customer has cut an order, and CoreWeave's results on Tuesday night were the clearest evidence yet that the AI build-out is still being paid for. This is a question about who runs the company, and nobody can tell you how the board answers it.

What to watch, and the two warnings that never change. The dates: Cisco and Applied Materials after the close tonight, both held inside QQQ, and the second of them an early read on whether chip factories are still being expanded; factory-gate prices tomorrow morning; Nvidia's own results on Tuesday, November 17; and the Fed on December 9, where some rate rise is now close to fully priced. Costco has two of its own: hold the shares on November 24 to receive the $30 a share special dividend, which is paid on December 4. First warning: four of your five are the same AI-chip bet — Nvidia owned outright, the largest single piece of both SPX and QQQ, and levered three times inside SOXL. Yesterday was the happy face of that, all four up together. This morning is the other face. Second warning: SOXL's daily reset, and the past five sessions are now the plainest example this page has ever carried. The chip index it triples rose about 3.5% over those five days. Three times that would be about +10.5%. SOXL fell about 1.0% — and that is after a day it gained 17.78%. It starts fresh every morning, so the 38.71% fall it took on November 5 shrank the pot that every later gain is calculated on, and the arithmetic never gives it back. To see its 1-year high again it needs about +357%. Nobody can tell you which way any of this resolves.
