Reasons it could go up

Set last week's fall against what caused it — borrowing costs at 19-year highs, oil above $93 with the Strait of Hormuz shut, and the market's biggest trade being repriced — and the index still gave back only 1.4%. The economy underneath keeps testing well: Friday's broad business survey was the strongest since April 2022, jobless claims fell to 206,000, and most of the store chains beat — Target with more customers walking in, Ross and BJ's too. The Treasury has now shown it will lean against the bond market, and its bigger buybacks actually begin September 9. And a down week takes some of the good news back out of prices: the bar for Wednesday's Nvidia report is lower than it was a week ago.

Reasons it could go down

Both of last week's pressures are still standing. Long-term rates ended the week near 19-year highs even after the Treasury stepped in — the relief lasted one day, and reports count the AI buildout's own borrowing among the things pushing rates up. Oil rose a second straight week with the Strait of Hormuz shut, and today's Iran announcement could tighten the squeeze further. The market's leadership is the other pressure: the AI shares fell all last week without any bad company news — technology was the worst group, down about 3% — and the index's biggest single company reports Wednesday. Walmart added a genuinely new worry: the country's biggest retailer said its customers are slowing down, and was marked down 9% in a day for it. Behind everything, a long-term government bond paying over 5% still competes with every expensive share in this index. And the new week opens with something new on the board: the trade deal with Canada fell through Friday night, and the 50% tariffs on about $20 billion a year of Canadian cars, cheese and alcohol are now in effect, with Canada's counter-tariffs set for September 8.
